# Daybook M2: Week and Day Views Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Add the day and week views beside the month: a Day / Week / Month switcher remembered per device, period navigation by the current unit, a time grid with an all-day strip that never truncates, side-by-side columns for overlapping events, and a current-time line on today.

**Architecture:** All arithmetic lives in `Packages/DaybookCore`: `Period` (which interval a view shows, stepping by its unit), `TimeGridLayout` (overlap columns for one day, all-day lanes for a day range) and a shared `SpanLanes` lane assigner that `MonthLayout` now uses too. `AppModel` stays the single owner of state (mode, focused date, loaded interval, layouts). One shared `App/TimeGrid/` draws the grid; `App/Week/WeekView.swift` and `App/Day/DayView.swift` are thin wrappers.

**Tech Stack:** Swift (language mode 5), SwiftUI, Swift Testing, XcodeGen, Xcode 26, iOS 26, macOS 26.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-17-daybook-design.md`, sections 4.1, 4.4, 6 and 7. This plan covers build step 2 of section 9.

**Scope notes for M2:**
- The switcher, period title, back / Today / forward and the Mac shortcuts ⌘1, ⌘2, ⌘3, ⌘T, ⌘←, ⌘→ arrive now. Search (⌘F) and "+ Event" (⌘N) stay with M5 and M3.
- Timed events shorter than 24 hours are drawn in the time grid, clipped to each day they touch. All-day events and timed events of 24 hours or more go to the all-day strip, like the system Calendar does. The month keeps its own rule (every multi-day event is a line).
- Clicking an event (popover) and creating events by double-click belong to M3.
- On iPhone the week is one horizontally paged strip of seven day columns, three per screen width, snapping to day boundaries; it opens with today (or the week's first day) at the leading edge.
- The day view's month navigator on Mac and iPad is the native graphical `DatePicker`.
- On iPhone the switcher sits in the bottom toolbar: the top bar already holds the calendars button, the title and back / Today / forward.

**Conventions:** as in the M1 plan. Run package tests with `swift test --package-path Packages/DaybookCore`. Build with `xcodegen generate`, then `xcodebuild -project Daybook.xcodeproj -scheme Daybook -derivedDataPath .build/xcode -quiet build` with `-destination 'platform=macOS'` and `-destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator'`. Work on branch `m2-week-day`.

### Task 1: Period arithmetic

**Files:**
- Create: `Packages/DaybookCore/Sources/DaybookCore/Layout/Period.swift`
- Test: `Packages/DaybookCore/Tests/DaybookCoreTests/PeriodTests.swift`

**Behaviour:** `ViewMode` (`day`, `week`, `month`, raw string values for `UserDefaults`). `Period.interval(_:containing:calendar:)`: a day is midnight to next midnight; a week is `calendar.dateInterval(of: .weekOfYear)`; a month is the full weeks that cover it (first day of `MonthLayout.weeks` to the day after the last). `Period.step(_:from:by:calendar:)` returns the start of the period `count` units away (day start, week start, month start), using calendar arithmetic only. `Period.days(in:calendar:)` lists the midnights inside an interval.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests**
  - day interval on 17 Nov 2026 is 24 h; on 25 Oct 2026 (Prague, clocks go back) it is 25 h and ends at 26 Oct midnight
  - week interval of 17 Nov starts Mon 16 Nov with `firstWeekday = 2` and Sun 15 Nov with `firstWeekday = 1`
  - week of 19 to 25 Oct 2026 is 7 days plus an hour and `days(in:)` returns seven midnights
  - month interval of November 2026 runs from 26 Oct to 7 Dec
  - `step(.day)` from 24 Oct by 1 is 25 Oct midnight, by 2 is 26 Oct midnight; by -1 from 29 Mar 2026 crosses spring DST to 28 Mar midnight
  - `step(.week)` from Wed 21 Oct by 1 is Mon 26 Oct midnight
  - `step(.month)` from 31 Jan 2026 by 1 is 1 Feb; by -1 from 15 Jan 2026 is 1 Dec 2025
  - `ViewMode(rawValue: "week") == .week`
- [ ] **Step 2: Run** `swift test --package-path Packages/DaybookCore`. Expected: build fails, `cannot find 'Period' in scope`.
- [ ] **Step 3: Implement `Period.swift`.**
- [ ] **Step 4: Run the tests.** Expected: all pass.

### Task 3: TimeGridLayout

**Files:**
- Create: `Packages/DaybookCore/Sources/DaybookCore/Layout/TimeGridLayout.swift`
- Test: `Packages/DaybookCore/Tests/DaybookCoreTests/TimeGridLayoutTests.swift`

**Behaviour:** `TimeGridLayout.isGridEvent(_:)` is true for timed events shorter than 24 h. `layout(day:events:calendar:minimumDuration:)` clips each grid event to the day, drops events that do not touch it, and returns `TimedPlacement`s (event, `startMinute`, `endMinute` as wall-clock minutes 0...1440, `column`, `columnCount`, `continuesBefore`, `continuesAfter`) sorted by start, longer first, then title. Overlap uses half-open intervals extended to `minimumDuration`, so events that touch end-to-start do not overlap. A cluster is a maximal run of transitively overlapping events; all its members share one `columnCount`; each takes the first free column. `allDaySegments(days:events:calendar:)` takes the non-grid events and lays them in lanes with `SpanLanes`.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests**
  - a lone event: column 0 of 1, minutes 540 to 600
  - two overlapping events: columns 0 and 1 of 2
  - touching end-to-start (9 to 10, 10 to 11): both column 0 of 1
  - transitive chain A 9:00 to 10:00, B 9:30 to 11:00, C 10:30 to 12:00: all count 2; A and C column 0, B column 1
  - three mutually overlapping events: columns 0, 1, 2 of 3
  - two separate clusters keep their own counts
  - 22:00 to 02:00 next day: on day one minutes 1320 to 1440 with `continuesAfter`; on day two 0 to 120 with `continuesBefore`
  - all-day and 24 h+ timed events are not in the grid but are in `allDaySegments`
  - two five-minute events back to back with `minimumDuration` of 30 minutes share a cluster of 2
  - wall-clock minutes on the DST day: 03:00 on 25 Oct 2026 is minute 180
  - all-day segments over a seven-day range: overlapping spans take lanes 0 and 1, a span starting before the range has `continuesBefore`
  - `splitByDay` of a three-column span returns three pieces, the middle one continuing both ways
- [ ] **Step 2: Run** the tests. Expected: build fails, `cannot find 'TimeGridLayout' in scope`.
- [ ] **Step 3: Implement.**
- [ ] **Step 4: Run** the tests. Expected: all pass.
- [ ] **Step 5: Commit** `Add the time grid layout`.
